# Field lessons

Treat these as hypotheses earned from prior use, then confirm against the user's current failures.

- The instruction file's own register is a style example. Rewrite literary or jargon-heavy configuration before adding another rule against literary or jargon-heavy output.
- Named artifacts act like slots eager models try to fill. Say that an empty mistakes log, discard list, or queue is valid.
- Short contradictions of host defaults have high behavioral pull. Duplicates and appeals such as “be thoughtful” have little value.
- Boundaries need unambiguous wording. Hedges are useful for preferences, harmful when they turn authority into guesswork.
- Layer by shelf life: identity/facts, working contract, and dated model-specific counterweights. Re-earn counterweights after major model changes.
- Repeated corrections are evidence for a standing line; repeated on-demand workflows are evidence for a skill. Do not encode universal numeric thresholds as law.
- Hooks are good for enforceable invariants, not a reliable cure for voice or register.
- A trap the model cannot predict must remain resident. A procedure deliberately invoked can move to a skill with a visible pointer.

Audit current transcripts only with user authorization and privacy controls. Counts support judgment; they do not replace it.
